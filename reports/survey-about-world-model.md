# Comprehensive Survey on World Models in Artificial Intelligence and Reinforcement Learning

## Executive Summary / TL;DR
World models—computational architectures that internalize the temporal and spatial dynamics of an environment to predict future states, actions, or outcomes—have emerged as a cornerstone of modern artificial intelligence and reinforcement learning (RL) [1]. Moving beyond reactive policy mapping, world models enable agents to simulate consequences, plan over long horizons, and learn efficiently inside internal "dreams" without exhaustive physical trial-and-error [2][3]. 

This survey synthesizes the paradigm shift from early variational and recurrent state-space models (e.g., Ha & Schmidhuber’s World Models, the Dreamer family) [2][3][4][5] to state-of-the-art generative video simulators, action-conditioned diffusion transformers, and joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) like Genie, Sora, and V-JEPA 2 [6][7][8]. Furthermore, we examine the critical evaluation landscape encompassing 102 representative benchmarks, distinguishing open-loop visual realism from closed-loop functional utility [9], and analyze persistent challenges such as compounding imagination drift, physical law hallucination [10], and latent collapse [1].

## Background & Motivation
The central tenet of model-based reinforcement learning and cognitive AI is that intelligent agents should not rely solely on trial-and-error interactions with the external world. Instead, building an internal simulator—a "world model"—allows an agent to imagine counterfactuals, anticipate obstacles, and evaluate action sequences prior to execution [2][3]. 

Historically, model-based methods struggled with high-dimensional sensory inputs such as raw pixels, often suffering from model bias and accumulated approximation errors over long trajectories. The modern era of world models bridges unsupervised representation learning, probabilistic forecasting, and scalable transformer/diffusion architectures. By compressing high-dimensional video streams into compact latent spaces and modeling transition dynamics conditioned on agent actions, modern world models achieve sample efficiencies orders of magnitude higher than model-free counterparts while unlocking zero-shot generalization across diverse digital and physical domains [2][3][7].

## Foundational Architectures & Early Milestones
The foundation of modern world models rests upon separating spatial perception from temporal dynamics and policy optimization [2]. 

### The V-M-C Paradigm and Early Recurrent Models
A seminal milestone was established by Ha & Schmidhuber (2018) with the **World Models** framework, which decomposed an agent into three sub-modules:
1. **Vision (V) model**: A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) that compresses high-dimensional raw pixel frames into a compact latent vector $z_t$ at each time step [2].
2. **Memory (M) model**: A Mixture Density Network combined with a Long Short-Term Memory (MDN-RNN) network that predicts the probability distribution $p(z_{t+1} \mid z_t, a_t)$ of future latent states, handling environmental stochasticity [2].
3. **Controller (C) model**: A compact policy mapping features $(z_t, h_t)$ to actions $a_t$, trained efficiently via Evolution Strategies (ES) entirely inside hallucinated dreams generated by the M model with temperature scaling $\tau$ [2].

### The Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) Family: PlaNet to DreamerV3
Building upon deterministic-stochastic factorization, Hafner et al. formalized the **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM)** across the *PlaNet* and *Dreamer* series [3][4][5]. RSSMs unify deterministic recurrent states $h_t$ and stochastic state representations $s_t$ to perform joint representation learning and trajectory prediction:
- **DreamerV1 (2019)**: Solved continuous visual control tasks purely by latent imagination using analytic gradients of learned state values backpropagated through predicted latent dynamics sequences [3].
- **DreamerV2 (2020)**: Replaced continuous Gaussian latent variables with discrete categorical variables (using a straight-through gradient estimator) and introduced KL balancing to prevent posterior collapse, successfully mastering all 58 Atari games on the 200M benchmark [4].
- **DreamerV3 (2023)**: Standardized robust scale-invariant training across diverse domains (Atari, Minecraft, continuous control, robotics) without hyperparameter tuning per environment, utilizing symlog transformations and two-hot categorical loss representations to handle massive reward and value scales [5].

## State-of-the-Art Generative & Predictive World Models
Recent advancements have scaled world models to internet video datasets and high-capacity transformer/diffusion backbones, bifurcating into pixel/token generative simulators and abstraction-focused predictive architectures [6][7][8].

### Generative Interactive Environments & Video Diffusion Simulators
- **Genie (2024)**: Developed by Google DeepMind, Genie is an 11B-parameter foundation world model trained unsupervised on 30,000 hours of unlabelled 2D platformer videos [6]. It comprises a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, a causal Latent Action Model (LAM) that infers discrete latent actions between frame pairs without action supervision, and an autoregressive MaskGIT dynamics model [6]. Genie enables frame-by-frame user interaction and zero-shot policy inference from action-free videos [6].
- **Sora (2024)**: OpenAI’s Sora demonstrated that large-scale text-conditional diffusion transformers (DiT) trained jointly on variable-duration videos and images can act as emergent world simulators [8]. By compressing raw video via a spatial-temporal VAE into spacetime patches treated as transformer tokens, Sora exhibits 3D spatial-temporal consistency, long-range object permanence across occlusions, and simulated digital game execution [8]. However, it remains prone to long-duration drift and occasional physical law violations [8].

### Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)
To bypass the computational overhead and noise sensitivity of pixel-level generation, self-supervised representation models like **V-JEPA 2 (2025)** predict dynamics entirely in learned abstract representation spaces [7]. Pretrained on over 1 million hours of internet video and aligned with robotic interaction datasets (e.g., Droid), V-JEPA 2 ignores unpredictable high-frequency pixel details (such as fluttering leaves or blades of grass) while precisely modeling predictable macro-dynamics (such as object trajectories and mechanical collisions) [7]. This enables zero-shot pick-and-place robot planning and action anticipation without task-specific environment fine-tuning [7].

## Evaluation Benchmarks & Functional Utility
The evaluation of world models has undergone a major transformation, moving beyond subjective visual realism to rigorous closed-loop functional validation [11][9].

### The Benchmark Landscape
Recent surveys categorize over 102 representative world model benchmarks across seven distinct evaluation targets [9]. Early efforts emphasized open-loop (OL) visual plausibility and physical reasoning (e.g., WorldModelBench, WorldPrediction) [11][9]. However, modern consensus highlights that generative realism does not imply physical understanding [11][1]. 

Consequently, closed-loop (CL) benchmarks—such as *WorldArena*, *Atari-100k*, *MineRL*, and *ManiSkill*—test feedback-driven interaction, action sensitivity, and policy success [1][9]. Studies reveal that 84.6% of functional utility benchmarks rely on closed-loop testing to verify whether an agent's internal world model genuinely aids decision-making and task completion [9].

## Trends, Open Problems & Future Horizons
Despite rapid progress, several foundational challenges constrain the broader deployment of world models [1]:

1. **Compounding Imagination Errors & Long-Horizon Drift**: As simulation horizons extend into the future, small prediction errors compound exponentially, causing latent states to drift into out-of-distribution regions.
2. **Hallucination of Physical Laws**: World models systematically exhibit hallucinations in low-data regimes, violating fundamental physical constraints (e.g., object permanence or gravity) [10]. While predictable and partially mitigatable via coverage-aware sampling [10], robust adherence to physics remains an active area of research.
3. **Latent-Space Collapse & Representation Drift**: In latent dynamics models, trivial solutions where distinct observations map to identical representations can destroy critical decision-making signals [1].
4. **Sim-to-Real Transfer & Action Scarcity**: While internet video provides abundant observation data, action-conditioned data remains scarce, creating bottlenecks when transferring simulated control policies to real robotic platforms [7][1].

Future horizons point toward hybrid architectures combining large-scale JEPA abstraction with precise causal reasoning engines, active data collection, and safety-guarded model predictive control to enable truly autonomous and robust embodied AI agents.
